Tree service in Garden Home and Whitford, Oregon

Garden Home began as a stop on the Oregon Electric Railway in the early 1900s, and the old rail grade is now the start of the Fanno Creek Trail at the Garden Home Recreation Center. The community spreads out from Oleson Road and Garden Home Road, with Whitford on its west side toward Highway 217.

The trees of Garden Home

ZIP 97223. Unincorporated Washington County, with Portland mailing addresses.

Homes range from prewar cottages to 1970s ranches, with new infill on divided lots. Almost all of it sits under tall second-growth Douglas-fir, and the two creeks that cross the area add a band of wet-ground trees.

The firs here came up after the area was logged more than a century ago, and they now stand well over a hundred feet above one-story houses. They were never thinned, so many grew as close groups with narrow crowns. Groups like that are stable together and much less so when one or two are removed from the middle.

Infill building is the newest pressure. When a deep lot is divided, the new foundation, driveway and utility trenches cut through the roots of trees on both the new lot and the neighbors'. The damage doesn't show for a few years, then appears as a thinning top or a tree down in a storm.

Along Ash Creek and Fanno Creek the trees are alder, cottonwood, willow and Oregon ash. They grow fast, fail young, and stand inside regulated corridors. With emerald ash borer now in Washington County, the ash are on borrowed time unless they are treated.

What we are usually called for in Garden Home

Firs next to new construction

Assessing and pruning trees whose roots were cut by a neighboring build, and removing the ones that have lost too much to stand safely. Tree removal.

Deadwood and roof clearance

Routine care for tall firs over older roofs: dead limbs out, low limbs off the shingles, a look at the top and the base. Tree trimming and maintenance.

Creek-side trees down in winter

Alders and cottonwoods that tip over on saturated ground along the creeks. We clear your side and explain what may have to stay in the corridor. Emergency tree service.

Stumps on lots being divided or built on

Anything under a future driveway or foundation is dug out and the hole compacted, not ground. Stump removal.

Around Garden Home

Local landmarks, and what the trees around each one tell you about your own.

Garden Home Recreation Center and the Fanno Creek Trail

The trail starts here on the old rail grade. It is lined with the area's typical trees, and the wet stretches show how alder and ash behave as they age: dead tops, leaning trunks and fallen limbs.

Oleson Road

The main north to south road is a county road with heavy traffic. Tree work beside it needs traffic control and a right-of-way permit when a lane is affected.

Ash Creek

The creek runs through the south side of Garden Home toward Tigard. Its banks carry a regulated vegetated corridor, and many back yards end inside it.

Whitford and the Highway 217 side

West toward the highway the streets are a little newer and the lots more regular. The boundary with Beaverton runs through this area, so neighbors a block apart can have different street tree rules.

Which tree rules apply in Garden Home

Garden Home and Whitford are unincorporated Washington County. Portland lies to the east, Tigard to the south and Beaverton to the west, each with a tree code of its own.

In unincorporated Washington County

No city tree code applies here, even with a Beaverton or Portland mailing address. The county generally leaves trees on a developed home lot to the owner, with two big exceptions: mapped Significant Natural Resource areas, and the vegetated corridors along creeks and wetlands that Clean Water Services regulates. Cutting inside either can need review, even for a dead tree.

Trees along the road are generally the adjoining owner's responsibility, and a county right-of-way permit is needed when planting or removal affects traffic. Many subdivisions add a layer of their own, with covenants and common-area trees that need the homeowners association's approval. For neighbor disagreements over trees, the county points residents to free community mediation.

Specific to Garden Home

Check which jurisdiction you are in if your street is near an edge. On the county side, ordinary yard trees are generally the owner's to manage. The exceptions are the Ash Creek and Fanno Creek corridors, which Clean Water Services regulates, and any tree a land division was required to protect. Lots being divided often come with tree protection conditions, so ask before clearing for a build.

The permit checker gives a first answer in two questions, and the permit guide has the detail. We check every tree's status before a job is booked.

Tree service questions from Garden Home

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Garden Home?

For an ordinary yard tree, usually not, since Garden Home is unincorporated county. Trees in the Ash Creek or Fanno Creek corridors are regulated, and a lot being divided may carry tree protection conditions. Check those two things first.

A new house is going in next door. Will it hurt my trees?

It can. Foundations, driveways and utility trenches inside the spread of your tree's branches cut its roots. Ask the builder where trenches will run, photograph your trees now, and have them looked at before and after the work.

My firs grow in a tight group. Can I remove just one?

Sometimes, but think about the rest. Firs that grew together shelter one another, and taking one from the middle or the windward side exposes the others. We look at the whole group before recommending which trees can go.

Is Garden Home part of Portland or Beaverton?

Neither. It is unincorporated Washington County with a Portland mailing address. Portland's city line runs along its east side, Beaverton's along the west and Tigard's to the south, so it is worth confirming near the edges.
