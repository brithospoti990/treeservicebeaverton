The first hour.

Pick what the tree has landed on.

On the house

Get everyone out of the rooms under the tree and keep them out: a trunk that has stopped moving can still settle. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 and NW Natural from outside. Turn off power to damaged rooms at the panel only if you can reach it safely.

Photograph the tree and the damage from the ground, inside and out. Then call us. We take the weight off the roof in pieces, from the top of the pile down, so the structure isn't loaded again while we work.

On a car or across the driveway

Look up before you walk up. If any wire is on or near the tree, treat this as a wires call. If someone is in a car with a line on it, PGE's advice is to stay inside until the line is dead.

With no wires involved, this is the simplest emergency we do. We cut an exit first, so you can get a vehicle out, and come back for the rest of the tree in daylight. Photograph the car before it's moved.

In the wires

Stay at least 50 feet away, keep neighbors and kids back, and call 911 and PGE at 503-464-7777. A line doesn't have to spark to be live, a wet tree conducts, and so does the ground around it.

We don't touch a tree that is in contact with a line, and neither should anyone else. Once PGE has cut the power and cleared the wire, the tree is ours to remove.

Hanging overhead

A limb that has cracked but not fallen is the one that hurts people, because it looks stable. It can come down days later on a calm afternoon. Rope off the ground underneath, move the cars, and keep the dog in.

We climb above it or reach it from the side, tie it off, and lower it under control. Don't pull on it with a rope, a pole saw or a pickup.

What counts as a tree emergency

During a storm everybody calls at once, so we work in order of danger, not in the order the phone rang. It helps to know where your tree falls on that list.

Call now

  • A tree or large limb on a house, garage or occupied building.
  • A tree blocking the only way in or out, especially for someone who needs medical access.
  • A cracked trunk, a split fork or a broken limb still hanging over a place people use.
  • A tree that has started to lean, with soil lifting or cracking at the base.
  • Anything in contact with wires, after you have called 911 and PGE.

Can wait for daylight

  • A tree down in the yard that hit nothing and blocks nothing.
  • Broken branches on the ground.
  • A fence panel under a limb, once the limb has stopped moving.

Those still need doing, and we do them. They just don't need doing at two in the morning, and work done in the dark is slower, riskier and harder on your yard.

What we ask on the phone

The address, what the tree is touching, whether wires are involved, whether anyone is still inside, and how we get a truck to it. A photo texted from a safe distance tells us which equipment to bring, which often saves a second trip.

Trees and wires: who fixes what

Three different parties repair three different things, in a fixed order. Knowing the order saves days.

  1. PGE makes it dead. They cut power to the line and clear or disconnect it. Until they have, nobody works on the tree.
  2. We remove the tree. With the line dead, we take the tree off the wire, the roof and the yard.
  3. Your electrician repairs the house side. If the mast was pulled off the wall or the meter base is bent, a licensed electrician fixes it, and an inspection may be needed before PGE reconnects.

If the mast has been torn loose, call the electrician the same day you call us. They are as busy as we are after a storm, and step three is the one that usually decides how long you are without power.

Insurance: what to photograph and what to keep

Call your insurer early, because most policies expect you to prevent further damage and will want to hear about it quickly. Then build the record an adjuster needs:

  • Wide photos that show the whole tree and what it hit, then close photos of each point of damage, inside and out.
  • A photo of the base of the tree. Whether it uprooted, snapped, or broke at an old decayed spot can matter later.
  • The date and time, and a screenshot of the weather warning if there was one.
  • Our itemized invoice, which separates making the site safe and taking the tree off the structure from hauling debris and grinding the stump. Insurers treat those differently.

In general, a policy pays to remove a tree that damaged a covered structure, and often one that blocks a driveway. It usually doesn't pay for a tree that fell on open lawn. We can't tell you what your policy says, but we do write the paperwork so the adjuster doesn't have to guess.

When it was the neighbor's tree

People expect the tree's owner to be responsible. Usually that's not how it works. When a sound tree comes down in a storm, each owner's own policy covers damage on their own side of the line. The neighbor is typically liable only if they knew, or should have known, that the tree was a hazard and did nothing. That is why a dated, written note to a neighbor about a dead or leaning tree is worth sending before the winter, not after.

Emergency removal and city permits

Beaverton's code recognizes that a tree in the right-of-way sometimes has to come out before a permit can be issued, when that is necessary to avoid imminent injury or damage. The same common sense applies to protected trees on private lots. Photograph the tree before and after, keep our invoice, and tell the Planning Division at 503-526-2420 once things are safe. Why Beaverton's storms break trees the way they do

JanuaryIce

Portland General Electric's report on the January 2024 storm named Beaverton, Tigard and Lake Oswego as the hardest-hit areas, with about 440,000 customers losing power over the week. Ice loads every twig at once. Long horizontal limbs on bigleaf maple and birch snap, twin trunks split, and arborvitae hedges splay open.

November to MarchWind on wet ground

By midwinter the valley's clay soils are saturated and hold roots poorly. A strong wind from an unusual direction, such as the east wind out of the Gorge, pushes on trees that grew up braced against southwest storms. That is when whole Douglas-firs go over, root plate and all.

July and AugustSummer limb drop

On hot, still afternoons, large limbs on oaks and maples sometimes fail with no wind at all. It is less common than storm damage and more surprising. Overextended limbs over patios and play areas are the ones to have shortened.
