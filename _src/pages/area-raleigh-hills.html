Tree service in Raleigh Hills, Oregon

Raleigh Hills grew up around the junction of Beaverton-Hillsdale Highway, Scholls Ferry Road and Oleson Road. Homes date from the 1940s through the 1960s, the lots are larger than almost anywhere else nearby, and many streets have no sidewalks and a rural feel that the trees have a lot to do with.

The trees of Raleigh Hills

ZIPs 97225 and 97221. Unincorporated Washington County, with Portland mailing addresses.

It is also a border community. Beaverton is a few blocks to the west and the Portland city line, which is also the county line, is a few blocks to the east. Three sets of tree rules operate within a short walk of each other.

Large lots grow large trees. Douglas-firs over a hundred and twenty feet are common, along with big western redcedars, bigleaf maples and a good number of mature ornamental trees planted by the first owners: copper beech, dogwood, Japanese maple and the occasional giant sequoia.

With room around them, many of these trees have full crowns down to the ground and long lower limbs, which is handsome and heavy. Long limbs over driveways and roofs are the usual concern, and end-weight reduction is usually the answer.

Fanno Creek begins in the hills just east of here and gathers several small tributaries through Raleigh Hills. Yards along those channels hold alder, ash and cottonwood on wet ground, inside corridors that are regulated.

Which tree rules apply in Raleigh Hills

Raleigh Hills is unincorporated Washington County. The Portland mailing address does not bring Portland's tree code with it, but the real city line is close.

In unincorporated Washington County

No city tree code applies here, even with a Beaverton or Portland mailing address. The county generally leaves trees on a developed home lot to the owner, with two big exceptions: mapped Significant Natural Resource areas, and the vegetated corridors along creeks and wetlands that Clean Water Services regulates. Cutting inside either can need review, even for a dead tree.

Trees along the road are generally the adjoining owner's responsibility, and a county right-of-way permit is needed when planting or removal affects traffic. Many subdivisions add a layer of their own, with covenants and common-area trees that need the homeowners association's approval. For neighbor disagreements over trees, the county points residents to free community mediation.

Specific to Raleigh Hills

If your street is near the county line, confirm which side you are on. Inside Portland, removing a private tree a foot or more across generally needs a city permit, which is far stricter than anything on the Washington County side. On the county side the tests are creek corridors, mapped natural resource areas and right-of-way work. State highways have their own permits.

The permit checker gives a first answer in two questions, and the permit guide has the detail. We check every tree's status before a job is booked.
